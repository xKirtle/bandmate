# A Nudge belongs to the Clip, not the Take

A Nudge is a Clip's: one stored value per Clip, layered on its window, moving whatever audio the Clip plays inside it. It was a Take's, which looked right — a Nudge corrects a recording's alignment, and recordings are Takes — but the per-Take granularity was never real. A new Take is placed at `CaptureStart - LatencyOffset`, so each Take's own hardware delay is already in its position; what a Nudge corrects is the calibration error left over, which belongs to the Input, not to the Take. Retake into the same Clip through the same Input and every Take wants the identical Nudge.

Holding it on the Take cost more than the granularity it bought. Nudging was a Take's alone, so the gesture that sets it — the nudge modifier held while dragging — applied to a Clip of Takes and was ignored on a Clip of a Beat or a Sound, where it fell through to a plain move: you asked for a nudge and got a move, on a Clip you may have meant to leave alone. As a Clip's, the gesture means one thing on every Clip, and the Clip menu's field reads the same number whatever the Clip holds. The alternative was to keep it on the Take and give a Beat Clip a second concept of its own for sliding audio inside a window; that buys a word ("slip") to explain for a mechanism the user can't tell apart.

Keeping the Nudge as **its own value** rather than deriving it was the other real choice. Deriving it is free in storage: nudging a Beat Clip is `offset` moving while `start` and `length` stay, and nudging a Clip of Takes is its Takes' positions shifting. It was rejected on two counts. The server refuses a Take starting before its Clip's source (`checkTakesAt`) and a window outside its file, so deriving it means allowing negative positions and offsets, at which point `offset` stops meaning "where in the source the window starts" and ADR 0023's earlier-as-later symmetry has nowhere to live. And a derived Nudge is unreadable: a nudged Clip becomes indistinguishable from one trimmed to the same place, so the correction can never be shown, typed or reset, and "the capture came in 40 ms late" is mixed into "this phrase belongs here" forever.

What a Nudge is for, given a move does nearly the same thing to the ear: it works where `clampMove` refuses a move, so a Clip wedged between its neighbours can still be aligned; it leaves the frame alone, so the Fades and the Cues over the Clip don't shift and the Timeline doesn't ask about them; it's in the audio's own time, so a later Tempo change doesn't drift it; and it keeps the correction separable from the placement. For one Clip in open space at 100% Tempo it is audibly a move — that is accepted, not overlooked.

The cost is a migration: `takes.nudge` becomes `clips.nudge`, and a Clip takes its active Take's value. Where a non-active Take had a different Nudge its audio shifts by the difference, which is silent until that Take is chosen. Downgrades cover the way back.
