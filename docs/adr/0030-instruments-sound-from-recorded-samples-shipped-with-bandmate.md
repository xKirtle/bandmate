# Instruments sound from recorded samples shipped with Bandmate

Hearing a Voicing plays recorded samples of its Instrument, not a sound synthesised in the browser: a few notes of a real instrument, each note heard played from the nearest one, pitch-shifted by its playback rate. The samples ship inside Bandmate, in the web app the binary embeds, and its own server serves them; nothing is fetched from a third party, so a self-hosted Bandmate sounds the same offline or behind a firewall. An Instrument names its sample set. The piano's is the Salamander Grand Piano (Alexander Holm, CC BY 3.0), every minor third from F#2 to C5, 11 files trimmed to 6 s, mono MP3 at 96 kb/s, about 800 KB; `web/scripts/samples.sh` remakes them from a pinned source, and their licence ships beside them. The guitar has its own set, and has lost its Karplus–Strong pluck: a nylon-string classical, the Raimundo 118 of the University of Iowa's Musical Instrument Samples (Brian Penkrot, 2011, free to use without restrictions), recorded string by string, frets 0, 3, 6, 9 and 12 of each string, 30 files retuned to A440 and trimmed to 4.5 s, mono MP3 at 96 kb/s, about 1.65 MB, made by the same script from Iowa's files pinned by checksum, its terms beside it. A guitar note plays its own string's nearest sample, so the same pitch sounds as its string does: a note up the neck on a thick string is warmer than the same note open on a thinner one, and two Voicings of a Chord that share pitches still sound apart.

Synthesis was decided first, because it costs nothing to download, works offline and needs no licence, and lost by ear (#1205). Three rounds of synthesised pianos were played beside the sampled grand: additive, modal and waveguide; then with a hammer knock, an uneven spectrum, a body and a room; then one fitted to the grand's measured envelope and spectrum, within 1–4 dB of it at middle C out to 4 s. Every one still read as plucked, and the samples read as a piano. Beside a real piano the synthesised guitar would be the one that sounded fake, which is why it goes too.

The guitar first played a steel-string set, one sample every 2 semitones whatever the string, from tonejs-instruments, which credits Iowa; but Iowa's only guitar is the nylon-string Raimundo, and the files match none of its recordings, so where they came from couldn't be verified. Playing one recording per pitch also lost what the pluck had, a note sounding darker up the neck of a thicker string. A low-pass by string and fret was tried on it and sounded muffled (#1209). No free steel-string guitar is recorded per string, so the guitar is nylon-string: a different guitar, accepted for the sound of each string.

The cost is accepted: the samples are about 0.8–1.7 MB a set in the binary and the image, an Instrument's set loads before it's first heard, which can fail, and the sets carry attribution, given in About, the self-hosting guide and the README. Loading is kept off the page's start: the device's Instrument's samples load when the Chord Finder or a Chord's popover first opens, and another's when it's picked. A Hear pressed before they arrive plays when they do, unless something else was pressed since, and if they can't load a toast says the Instrument couldn't be heard and the next press tries again. There is no synthesised fallback: a fallback would be a second sound to keep matched and tuned, for a case a retry covers.

That toast extends ADR 0029's stack beyond refused saves, for the same reason the stack exists: a Hear button is an icon in a card or a popover, with nowhere beside it for a line to go. On the Song page it joins the refused saves, labelled with the Instrument rather than a surface, so five at most; on the Chord Finder's page it's the stack's only toast. Like a refused save it's never on a timer: it goes when the samples load, when it's dismissed, or when its page is left.
