# Instruments sound from recorded samples shipped with Bandmate

Hearing a Voicing plays recorded samples of its Instrument, not a sound synthesised in the browser: a few notes of a real instrument, each note heard played from the nearest one, pitch-shifted by its playback rate. The samples ship inside Bandmate, in the web app the binary embeds, and its own server serves them; nothing is fetched from a third party, so a self-hosted Bandmate sounds the same offline or behind a firewall. An Instrument names its sample set. The piano's is the Salamander Grand Piano (Alexander Holm, CC BY 3.0), every minor third from F#2 to C5, 11 files trimmed to 6 s, mono MP3 at 96 kb/s, about 800 KB; `web/scripts/samples.sh` remakes them from a pinned source, and their licence ships beside them. The guitar is to get its own set, a steel-string, and lose its Karplus–Strong pluck.

Synthesis was decided first, because it costs nothing to download, works offline and needs no licence, and lost by ear (#1205). Three rounds of synthesised pianos were played beside the sampled grand: additive, modal and waveguide; then with a hammer knock, an uneven spectrum, a body and a room; then one fitted to the grand's measured envelope and spectrum, within 1–4 dB of it at middle C out to 4 s. Every one still read as plucked, and the samples read as a piano. Beside a real piano the synthesised guitar would be the one that sounded fake, which is why it goes too.

The cost is accepted: the samples are about 0.8 MB a set in the binary and the image, an Instrument's set loads before it's first heard, which can fail, and the sets carry attribution, given in About, the self-hosting guide and the README. Loading is kept off the page's start: the device's Instrument's samples load when the Chord Finder or a Chord's popover first opens, and another's when it's picked. A Hear pressed before they arrive plays when they do, unless something else was pressed since, and if they can't load a toast says the Instrument couldn't be heard and the next press tries again. There is no synthesised fallback: a fallback would be a second sound to keep matched and tuned, for a case a retry covers.
