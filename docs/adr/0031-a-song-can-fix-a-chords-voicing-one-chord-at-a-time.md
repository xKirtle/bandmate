# A Song can fix a Chord's Voicing, one Chord at a time

A Chord can carry a Song Voicing: a guitar Voicing written after its name, inside the same brackets, as `[Em:00002x]` or `[Em:x-0-9-9-x-x]`. On the guitar, untransposed, it's drawn in the Chord Chart and the Chord's popover in place of the device's preferred Voicing. Its frets count from the capo, on the Song's tuning, and stay as written if the tuning changes; one on more or fewer strings than the tuning gets no diagram rather than a guess.

This bends ADR 0012, where every Voicing is generated for the tuning in use and the one a player likes is a preference kept on each device. That's still how a Chord is played by default. But a preference is about the player and holds for every Song, while some Songs are about one way of playing a Chord: open strings ringing against fretted notes up the neck, a Voicing on only some strings, a particular inversion. That belongs to the Song, so it travels with it to every device and into a Backup, and it can't be a preference without changing every other Song with that Chord. The search may not even find it: a generated Voicing keeps to the Chord's tones, four frets and four fingers, which a written one needn't. So a Song now holds Voicings, but only ones written for it, never generated ones.

A Song Voicing belongs to its one Chord, not to a Song-wide definition of the name, as ChordPro's `{define}` gives. With a definition, writing it changes every Chord of that name in the Song, including ones written before it and ones whose player meant the usual Voicing; with the Voicing on the Chord, no Chord changes because of one written elsewhere, and a verse and a chorus can play the same name two ways. The cost is repeating the frets on each Chord played that way, accepted as the price of never having to look elsewhere to know how a Chord is played. The Chord Chart draws one diagram per distinct way a name is played.

It's written inline, after the name, rather than in a list of the Song's Voicings of its own, for the same reason: what's written with the Chord is all there is to it, it moves with the Chord when its Line is edited, moved or copied, and there's nothing apart from the Lyric Sheet to keep in step with it. Read mode and Print show only the name, so the frets don't clutter the Line, while Write mode shows the brackets as typed. Bracketed text whose name or frets can't be read stays in the lyrics, as any bracket that isn't a Chord does, so a typo shows at once rather than being dropped (ADR 0008).

It falls away under Transpose and on the piano, where the Chord is drawn like any other of its name. A Song Voicing is how the Song is played in its own key, on a guitar: moved up or down, a fixed Voicing may not be playable, or may not be the colour it was chosen for, and Transpose is there to give playable Voicings for the new key. A piano has no frets to follow. Moving a Song Voicing under Transpose, and Song Voicings for the piano, were left out.
